## 1. Executive Summary

**You and Yacine Mouloudi are co-founding one new company together.** This is not a partnership between two separate businesses — it is a single venture with two founders who bring complementary skills.

| Co-founder | Primary strength | Typical focus |
|------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Yacine Mouloudi** | Audience, trust, marketing, Algeria-side student ops | Content, office, local paperwork, community |
| **You (HE Consultant)** | University access, admissions expertise, Hungary-side knowledge | Professor meetings, agent agreements, process design |

**Origin of the idea:** Yacine previously promoted for an **existing agency**. He built that pipeline — the audience, the trust, the ~100 students per semester. He is leaving to co-found **your company** with you. From launch, he promotes **your brand, for both of you** — not the old agency.

**Yacine's track record = your company's credibility.** These are his merits, and they come with the new venture:
- ~100 students facilitated per semester *(confirmed — Yacine)*
- ~1M social media followers *(confirmed — founders)*
- Post-2022 growth in Algerian demand for Budapest *(confirmed — Yacine message)*
- Proven end-to-end process: office → registration → acceptance → visa → arrival
- Algerian student community already in Budapest

**For students, Yacine's name is the brand.** They know him, trust him, and will follow him to your company. The legal company name and website sit behind that — but **Yacine is the concept students recognize**.

**The old agency** loses Yacine as its promoter and likely becomes a competitor — but **you keep everything he built** in terms of reputation, numbers, and audience.

**Revenue model (typical industry):**
- University commission per enrolled student (after refund deadline)
- Optional consulting/service fee paid by student (must be transparent)
- **Not** paid intermediation for Stipendium Hungaricum (official scholarship — free, government-managed)

**One-line mission:**  
*Guide Algerian students from first curiosity to enrolled student in Hungary — with clarity, community, and zero confusion about costs or paths.*

## 2. Business Model (How It Works)

```
Algerian student sees Yacine content
        ↓
Free online orientation (webinar / video series)
        ↓
Decision: "I want Hungary"
        ↓
Paid or structured consultation → university + program match
        ↓
Document checklist → application → acceptance
        ↓
Visa / residence permit support
        ↓
Arrival in Budapest → registration + settling in
```

### Two student paths (keep separate in all communication)

| Path | Who runs it | Our role |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Stipendium Hungaricum** (government scholarship) | Tempus Public Foundation + Algerian sending partner | Free guidance only — explain official steps, link to official portal, never charge for application |
| **Self-funded / university contract** | Our consultancy + partner universities | Full service — consultation, application, visa, arrival |

**Critical rule:** Never mix these paths in marketing. Charging for scholarship applications damages trust and may violate program rules.

## 3. Competitive Landscape

*All competitor notes below are from **public websites** (market research). Not verified internal data.*

### 3.1 Official reference — Study in Hungary / Stipendium Hungaricum

**What they are:** Government program (Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Tempus Public Foundation). Not a competitor — the **authority**.

**What they do well:**
- Strong country brand: "Crossroads of Europe"
- 90+ sending partners, 600+ programs, credible scholarship story
- Free online application system (DreamApply)
- Clear warning: *"Do not apply through unknown agencies"*

**What we learn:**
- Use Hungary's official credibility in our content (facts, links)
- Never position ourselves as the scholarship provider
- Our value = navigation + support for paths that **are** allowed to use agents

**What we must NOT copy:**
- Claiming official status we don't have
- Charging for Stipendium applications

---

### 3.2 Hungary Education Gateway (Ghana / UK model)

**Website:** hungaryeducationgateway.com

**What they do well:**
- Clean, simple value proposition: Quality / Experience / Cost / Career
- Regional focus (West Africa) — speaks to one audience
- Physical office + contact form + WhatsApp culture
- University partner logos for credibility

**What they do poorly / gaps:**
- Generic copy — could be any country
- No community or influencer layer
- No visible student journey map (step-by-step)
- Limited social proof (few testimonials visible)

**What we do differently:**
- Algeria-specific content (Arabic + French + English)
- Influencer trust layer they cannot replicate
- Show real student stories from Algerian community in Budapest

---

### 3.3 Student Guide Hungary (Budapest-based)

**Website:** sghungary.com

**What they do well:**
- Strong tagline: **"Study in Hungary. Simplified"**
- Clear service blocks: University Selection → Application → Visa
- Niche focus: Medical entrance exam prep (smart specialization)
- WhatsApp-first contact (matches Algerian student behavior)
- Budapest address = local trust

**What they do poorly / gaps:**
- Website feels incomplete / truncated
- No origin-country office story
- Generic international positioning, not country-specific
- No content marketing funnel visible

**What we do differently:**
- Founder-led: influencer + HE consultant in **one** company (not outsourced marketing)
- Algeria office + Hungary expertise under **one brand**
- Community-first, not just "simplified paperwork"

---

### 3.4 Studies in Hungary / Studeo Hungaria

**Website:** studiesinhungary.com

**What they do well:**
- Broad service stack: admission, immigration, housing, job assistance
- Course catalog by field (Medicine, IT, Business, etc.)
- B2B agent network (India, UK offices) — commission model documented
- "Call for Partners" page — recruits sub-agents
- Hungarian company positioning: "We represent local universities"

**What they do poorly / gaps:**
- Typos and duplicate content hurt professionalism
- Overloaded service list — hard to know where to start
- Feels like a directory, not a guided journey
- No strong brand personality

**What we do differently:**
- Fewer services on day one, executed excellently
- One clear funnel: Orient → Consult → Apply → Arrive
- Premium brand feel (influencer + consultant credibility)

---

### 3.5 HunEducation (long-established Hungary agency)

**What they do well:**
- 24+ years experience — deep trust signal
- Positions as "official application center" for partner universities
- Detailed arrival services (airport pickup, bank account, SIM, orientation)
- Multi-language site

**What they do poorly / gaps:**
- Old-school web presence
- Turkey-focused historically; not Algeria-specific
- Service list is long — overwhelming for first-time student

**What we learn:**
- Arrival support is a **retention and referral driver** — don't skip it
- "Complete package" sells, but must be phased (see Section 5)

---

### 3.6 The old agency Yacine promoted for (what we leave behind)

**What Yacine built there — now yours to claim:**
- The ~100 students/semester result
- The influencer-driven acquisition model
- Algeria office workflow and student support process
- Trust of Algerian students who follow Yacine

**What changes:** Yacine stops promoting them. He promotes **your company** exclusively. His name, audience, and track record move with him.

**The old agency** likely becomes a competitor without its main channel — but **you are not starting from zero**. You start with Yacine's full merit and momentum; you add your university access and a company you own together.

## 4. What We Might Be Getting Wrong or Overdoing

| Risk | Why it's a problem | What to do instead |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Too many universities too fast** | Hard to support quality; weak commission deals | Start with 2–3 partner universities, nail the process, then expand |
| **Copying generic "Study in Hungary" copy** | Sounds like every other agency | Lead with Algerian student stories, Budapest life, Yacine's journey |
| **Offering Stipendium as a paid service** | Illegal feel, destroys trust, official program warns against it | Free info only; monetize self-funded path |
| **Building Hungary office before Algeria funnel works** | Ops cost without volume | Lean ops first: Yacine handles Algeria-side; you handle university side |
| **Forgetting to re-sign university agent deals** | No commission legally | Register tracker codes under your company name — Yacine's volume helps the pitch |
| **Launching before Yacine's exit is clean** | Non-compete lawsuit, brand clash | Phase 0: legal clearance + transition announcement plan |
| **Overpromising visa success** | Visas depend on embassy, documents, student profile | Clear disclaimers; document checklist as product |
| **Website before offer is clear** | Beautiful site, confused customer | Define packages, pricing logic, and 2–3 programs first |
| **Ignoring French & Arabic** | Most Algerian students search in FR/AR, not EN | Trilingual content from day one (EN for universities, FR/AR for students) |
| **Skipping founders' agreement** | Equity fights, exit chaos | Written co-founder agreement: equity, roles, vesting, decision rights |
| **Treating Yacine as "marketing only"** | He built the demand — he must be co-owner in story and ops | Both founders on About page; shared decision on pricing and brand |
| **Two brands at launch** | Students don't know who to trust | One company, one name, one website — period |

## 7. Website & Marketing — Do / Don't Quick Reference

### DO
- Show the step-by-step journey visually
- Use Yacine's real story and face
- Be explicit about costs (tuition, living, service fees)
- WhatsApp as primary CTA
- Separate scholarship vs self-funded paths
- Show Algerian students already in Budapest

- Promise guaranteed admission or visa
- Use stock photos of random students
- Copy competitor text word-for-word
- List 20 universities before signing 3
- Charge for Stipendium application support
- Launch in English only
- Hide who you are (no faces, no address, no contact)
